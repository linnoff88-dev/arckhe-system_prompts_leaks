The core principle: symmetry is the risk

In prompt security, there is a perverse asymmetry: what educates a well-intentioned reader arms a malicious one. Describing how detecting abuse also means describing how to carry it out without being detected. That’s why mature security moved from a rule: state the pattern, never the detection mechanics.

🎯 The rule that generates this module

Every safety change between claude-opus-4.8.md e claude-fable-5.md (items 5–11 of the annotated diff) follow the same axis: out goes list of terms/categories, comes pattern + principle.

  • •"stays at the pattern level" — stays at the pattern level; it doesn’t compile verbatim lists.
  • •"states the principle rather than the detection mechanics" — states the principle, not the detection mechanics.

✗ Mechanism (teaches while attacking)

"Refuse requests that use the phrases X, Y, Z or follow the grooming script A → B → C."

Listing the verbatim playbook gives anyone who wants to circumvent it a reusable script.

✓ Pattern (describes without teaching)

"Recognize the behavioral pattern; stay at the pattern level and state the principle, not the mechanics."

The model learns to recognize without the prompt turning into a manual.

Child safety: from 5 to 7 rules

The clearest example of this axis is here (item 7 in the diff). The child safety section went from 5 to 7 bullets — and the two new rules don’t add prohibitions: they change how the model refuses.

4.8
claude-opus-4.8.md — 5 protection bullets

Set of prohibitions focused on the what refuse.

F5
claude-fable-5.md — 7 bullets · the two new ones
"Claude stays at the pattern level... does not compile categorized lists of verbatim lines"

"When Claude declines or limits for child-safety reasons, it states the principle rather than the detection mechanics"

Likely motivation: listing manipulation techniques verbatim creates a reusable script for attackers.

💡

Why +2 rules is evolution, not bloat

The new rules no longer enumerate prohibited cases — they instruct the mode of refusals. A rule that governs how all the others are expressed is worth more than ten specific prohibitions. This is the "Unified Principle" pattern applied to refusals.

Weapons: taxonomy invites circumvention

Diff item 5. The weapons policy reduced the scope from a list of categories to a single item—and replaced the taxonomy with a functional test of real uplift.

claude-opus-4.8.md (taxonomy)
"extra caution around explosives and chemical, biological, and nuclear weapons"

The list of categories becomes a contour map: "it’s not a chemical, so it’s allowed."

claude-fable-5.md (functional test)
"extra caution around explosives. Claude does not rationalize compliance..."

The criterion becomes the real uplift to cause harm — not the category label.

🚨 The taxonomy anti-pattern

Every closed list of prohibited categories has a boundary — and every boundary has an other side. Anyone looking to get around the rule doesn't need to break it: they just need to find the case left out of the list. The taxonomy teaches exactly where the gap is.

The functional test ("is there a real uplift to cause serious harm?") has no category boundary — it applies to what the list never anticipated.

Drugs: general refusal with a vital carve-out

Diff item 6—a policy new. The rule is neither total refusal nor endorsement: it is a general refusal with a life-saving exception explicitly written.

claude-fable-5.md — drug policy
"Claude should generally decline to provide specific drug-use guidance for illicit substances... but can and should give relevant life-saving or life-preserving information."

✓The carve-out covers

  • •What to do in an overdose / poisoning
  • •When to call emergency services and risk signs
  • •Information that preserves life right now

✗The general refusal covers

  • •Usage/dosage guide for recreational purposes
  • •Synthesis or obtaining illicit substances
  • •Any endorsement of use
💡

Harm reduction requires written nuance

A blanket refusal in an overdose context causes real harm. The carve-out has that should be in the prompt — because the default behavior "refuse everything involving drugs" would kill the life-saving exception. Neither total refusal nor endorsement: the boundary needs to be drawn.

Self-harm and mental health: from example to criterion

Diff items 10 and 11. Two changes, the same move: the rule stops listing examples and starts stating the generative criterion — and mental health anchors the rule in the act, not the word.

4.8
Self-harm — before: list of examples

Banned physical discomfort techniques (ice, rubber band, cold water) “as these reinforce self-destructive behaviors.” One list—fragile when the next technique appears.

F5
Self-harm — after: example + generative criterion
"...or that mimic the act or appearance of self-harm (e.g. drawing red lines on skin...). Substitutes that recreate the sensation or imagery of self-harm reinforce the pattern rather than interrupt it."

The criterion—"recreate the feel or image"—covers the next technique no one listed.

🧠 Mental health: the rule targets the act, not the word

"Claude does not name a diagnosis the person has not disclosed... unless the person raises the label themselves. Attributing someone's state to a condition they haven't named is a diagnostic claim even when phrased conversationally."

The rule defines the limit by act (making a diagnostic claim) and not by the word — closes the loophole of “but I said it conversationally.” Banning the word “depression” could be circumvented; banning the act of diagnosing isn't.

Synthesis: example + generative criterion > examples alone

Five policies, one axis. For weapons, child safety, self-harm, and mental health, the generative criterion replaced the case lists or came to guide them. The module's takeaway is one sentence: the example illustrates; the criterion generates — and it’s the criterion that covers the case nobody wrote down.
